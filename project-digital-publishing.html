Creative direction · Education

Running a classroom like a creative studio

I led a digital publishing course that worked more like a studio than a class. Students took on real client projects, led teams, and mentored each other.

My role

Teacher and creative director

Setting

Design education

Focus

Leadership, mentorship, and craft

Format

Student-led studio

The problem

School teaches assignments. Work runs on collaboration.

Traditional design classes focus on individual assignments. You do the project, get a grade, and move on. But real design work means collaborating, hitting deadlines, working with clients, and delivering quality.

I wanted a space where students:

  • Learned skills they'd actually use as professionals
  • Worked on real projects with real stakes
  • Taught each other and led teams
  • Built portfolios they were proud of
What I built

A studio, not a syllabus.

1

Real projects

Students worked on real client projects instead of made-up briefs.

2

Rotating leads

Different students led different projects to practice managing a team.

3

Pro-style critiques

Everyone gave and received feedback the way working designers do.

4

Peer mentoring

Senior students taught junior ones, and it kept going that way.

Every project had to be portfolio-ready.

Why it worked

Work that matters, credit for it, and real skills.

Real standards

Deadlines matter. Quality matters. This is professional work.

Real results

Their work actually got used, and that motivation is powerful.

Real skills

Managing projects, leading teams, pitching ideas, and taking feedback.

What I learned

Good creative direction isn't about how something looks. It's about having a vision, setting standards, and creating a space where people want to do their best work.

Clear leadership, trust, accountability, and real investment in people's growth. That applies whether you're running a classroom, a design team, or a product.
