Resources V2

Rebuilding navigation from scratch, mid-reorg.

There was no old version to reference, stakeholders wanted different things, and the launch window was tight.

Sales teams use Resources to find the materials they need, but the old experience was messy and hard to trust. With a fellow designer, I rebuilt the information architecture, navigation, and content templates from the ground up.

  • Strategy: A brand-new information architecture, with no blueprint to copy.
  • Research: Interviews with sales engineers and account executives about what they actually need.
  • Personas and flows: Built to guide every design decision.
  • Prototyping: Wireframes and prototypes, tested and iterated.
  • Shipping: Partnered with developers, ran a QA bug bash, and kept stakeholders in the loop.

New features included filter pills, multi-select filters, expanding side panels, and "official" stamps so people knew which content to trust.

The result: Resources became the second most-clicked destination in Solutions Workspace.

Demo Signups

Research

Is the platform working, and are sellers ready for AI?

Leadership wanted to know how well Solutions Workspace was working and whether sellers were ready for AI. I contributed to a large study with the design team:

32

Sales engineers

179

Account executives

9

Stakeholder interviews

What we found

  • Usage is concentrated: AEs spend most of their time in Value and Demos.
  • Discoverability is the gap: The top AE pain point was "I don't know where to start or what's available."
  • AEs are ready for AI: They want automation that cuts manual effort.
  • Enablement matters: Better onboarding would drive adoption of features that already exist.
Content templates

Not every piece of content should look the same.

Apps, demos, and assets all used the same layout, which made things harder to find and use. I led the problem framing and designed a template for each content type.

  • Interviewed content authors and tested wireframes with sales engineers.
  • Ran a FigJam AI workshop and led enablement sessions with the content team.
  • Shipped templates for App, Demo Environment, and Asset, then extended them to Services.
  • Built a spec'd Figma production template and reusable components, like a stepper and an accordion.
  • Trained a non-design team to use Figma so they could keep the system going.
What I learned

Handoff is a design problem too.

Strategy and design need to be in the room from the beginning. And handoff isn't where design ends. Sit with the receiving team until the system works for how they operate.
