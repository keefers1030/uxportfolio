    An agent that finds the rep who already won a deal like yours.

    Phone a Friend is a skill inside Salesforce's Sales Agent. A seller describes the deal they're working, and the agent finds peers who closed similar deals, or customers who could serve as a reference, right in Slack. I owned the UX design and testing, from the UX brief through the V1 launch.

    01 · The problem

    The right advice already existed. Finding it took a request.

    When sellers try to win a deal, they look for other sellers who faced a similar situation, for advice or as a customer reference. Before the agent, that meant digging through Slack channels or asking a team to do the digging for you.

      UX principles · and how each one shows up in the flow
      01

      Efficiency

      Sellers are mid-deal. The agent finds matches from the opportunity itself, so the seller doesn't have to type out every detail.

      02

      Accuracy

      Before it searches, the agent confirms which opportunity and which product the seller means.

      03

      Clarity

      Every match comes with who they are, their role, and which criteria matched, so the seller knows what to do next.

      03 · The flow

      One question up front, then two paths.

      The MVP flow opens with a single question: what kind of help are you looking for? The answer sends the seller down one of two search paths, or hands them off to customer stories. Pick one to see what the agent does.

      MVP flow · pick a path

        Rebuilt from the MVP flow. Peer support and customer reference results are both stored for reporting.

        Peer support

        Matches on the deal

        The agent looks at the opportunity's products, industry, and segment, and finds closed/won opportunities that match. It returns up to six, with the criteria that matched, the opportunity owner's name, and their role.

        Customer reference

        Matches on the account

        The agent first tells the seller what it will match on and asks if there's anything to add. Then it searches accounts and returns up to six, with the account name, owner, products, and industry.

        From MVP to V2 · working notes from the V2 flow

        What would we track?

        V2 added a list of things we'd want to track, with a note that the Customer Reference team could own the monitoring.

        • The seller's opportunity: size, products, industry, stage, region, and whether it closed and for how much
        • Whether the agent helps shorten the sales cycle
        • Which accounts and account owners were suggested
        • Who the seller actually used, so the agent can learn which references work

        How should references be matched?

        Many of these were still questions for the team at this stage.

        • Match on the account's product footprint, which can include more than one product
        • Use license usage to gauge how far the customer has implemented
        • Don't share accounts that are red on any product
        • The account owner must be an active person
        • Product should be a full match. Region or industry is nice to have
        • SMB sellers get SMB answers, and other sellers don't
        04 · The conversation

        Designing how the agent talks, not just what it finds.

        The agent had to turn a loose Slack message into a precise search, and recover gracefully when it wasn't sure. The work moved through dialogue mapping, prompt and fallback design, and seller research.

        From message to match · a conceptual model
        Layer 1 · Conversational UI

        Plain language in

        Seller in Slack"Hey Agent, I'm working on a competitive replacement pitch for Acme Corp. Who is the technical specialist that ran that account?"

        Unstructured natural language input.

        Layer 2 · Intent and constraint

        Turn it into something searchable

        A · Intent classification"Who is the technical specialist..." becomes the intent Find_Deal_Specialist
        B · Entity extraction"Acme Corp" becomes the parameter [Account_Name]
        Fallback matrixIf confidence is low, the agent checks with the seller: "Did you mean Acme Corp or Acme Holdings?"

        Prompt optimization and fallback state design. UX and tech collaboration.

        Layer 3 · CRM architecture

        Map to real records

        Account.Name → Acme Corp
        Opportunity.Stage → Filter closed-won
        Opportunity.OwnerID → Active specialist profile

        Deterministic schema mapping.

        A conceptual model from my portfolio deck. Acme Corp and the intent name are examples, not real data.

        Rules the conversation had to follow · from the BRD

        Ask when it's unclear

        If the seller doesn't name an opportunity, the agent asks for an industry, competitor, product, or segment. If it can't tell which opportunity, it lists them. On an opportunity record, "this record" works.

        Ask about products

        If the opportunity has no products, or several, the agent asks which one. Sellers can name more than one.

        Admit when there's no match

        When nothing fits, the agent says it can't locate a resource, asks how it can improve, and points to a feedback form.

        Stay in its lane

        Customer story requests go to the Customer Story topic. Competitor strengths, weaknesses, and pricing questions are out of scope for this skill.

        Share only what's safe

        Closed/won deals from the last two years, with active people as owners. SMB sellers see SMB references, and other sellers don't.

        Order and role

        Results run best match first, up to six. Each shows which criteria matched, and a later requirement added the other seller's role.

        07 · Retrospective

        From designing tools to designing the conversation.

        The shift

        Designing the interaction

        This project grew my expertise in human-agent collaboration. I moved from designing tools for users to designing the interaction between a seller and an AI agent.

        What I'd repeat

        Read the requirements first

        I joined after the project started and used the BRD to get up to speed before the first UX scoping call.

        What I'd repeat

        Raise the hard questions early

        Opt-out, data reliability, a feedback loop, and phasing went into the UX brief, so engineering could work through them before build.

        What I learned

        The best AI products? You don't even think about the AI. It just works.
