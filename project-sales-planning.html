Grounding AI in Sales Strategy

Building Trust and Velocity in High-Stakes Planning

Role
UX Research & Strategy
Type
Foundational Research
Duration
6 months
Scope
20+ Enterprise Teams

Overview

I spent six months researching how sales teams can actually use AI for planning. The goal was figuring out what would make them trust AI and what would make them use it.

The work changed how the entire product team thinks about AI in sales. It shaped multiple features and gave us a playbook for building AI that people actually want.

The Question

Sales leaders are careful about AI. They deal with big decisions that affect the company's money. The question was simple: how do we build AI that they actually trust?

I needed to understand not just what they do, but why. What makes them confident. Where they see AI helping versus where they see it as a risk.

How I Researched It

Interviews

Deep conversations with 20+ sales leaders. I wanted to understand how they actually plan and what they trust.

Observation

Sat in on planning meetings. Watched how teams make decisions, where they get stuck, where they might benefit from AI.

Workflow Mapping

Traced their planning process to find the friction points. These are where AI could actually help.

Concept Testing

Tested AI concepts with them to see what they liked and what made them nervous.

Key Insights

1. Show Your Work

Leaders need to understand why AI gave that recommendation. A smart prediction is worthless if you can't explain it to your team.

2. Let People Decide

AI that makes decisions for people gets rejected. AI that makes suggestions and lets people choose gets used. Even if AI is more accurate, people want a say.

3. Make It Relevant

Generic AI suggestions feel useless. AI that gets your specific deals, your team, your market—that's valuable.

4. Speed Plus Confidence

People want AI to help them work faster without making them less confident in their decisions. The design challenge is doing both.

5. People Follow People

When leaders see other leaders they respect using AI, adoption takes off. Seeing it work matters more than hearing it's good.

Impact on Product Direction

This research directly shaped the design of multiple AI features:

  • → Explainability Layers: Every AI recommendation includes clear reasoning and source data
  • → Human-in-the-Loop Workflows: AI suggests, but humans decide and execute
  • → Contextual Intelligence: AI learns team-specific patterns and preferences
  • → Success Stories: In-product features highlight how other teams are using AI successfully

What This Taught Me

Research changes how you build things. It's not just about saying "good idea" or "bad idea." It's about finding the truth that changes everything.

Without this research, we might have built something technically smart that nobody wanted. By understanding how leaders actually think and decide, we built AI that was powerful and trustworthy.
