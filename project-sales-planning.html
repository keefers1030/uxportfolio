Research That Changed the Sales Planning Roadmap

Nobody was asking sellers how they actually use these tools

Role
UX Researcher & Program Owner
Type
Foundational Research
Scope
Global Research Initiative
Impact
FY27 Roadmap, Recurring Program

The Problem

Account Plans and QAPs are critical tools for sellers. They use them every day. But nobody on the team actually understood how they worked or where sellers got stuck.

The March QAP cycle was approaching. Nobody had flagged this gap. Nobody owned it. And if we didn't understand seller behavior, we'd be guessing on the FY27 roadmap.

I spotted the gap. I spotted the deadline. And I built a research plan from scratch to answer the questions nobody was asking.

Global research coordination across three continents and time zones, targeting sellers at every level—from individual contributors to sales leaders.

What Sellers Told Us

Planning felt like a guessing game. That one quote said everything. Sellers weren't thinking strategically. They were filling out forms they had to fill out and hoping their numbers made sense.

What I Built

I built the whole research initiative from scratch. Designed the study. Recruited 38 sellers across three continents. Coordinated the sessions. Translated the findings. Got them to the right people in time.

Three key insights emerged. Planning isn't in the planning tools. Most of it happens in spreadsheets and emails. And there's a massive lag between when data's available and when sellers can actually use it. That gap forces them to work around the system.

1
Spotted the Gap
The March cycle was coming. Nobody else saw the research gap or the deadline. I built a plan around both to make sure findings would land in time for roadmap decisions.
2
Designed the Study
Asked the questions that actually mattered. How do sellers really use these tools? Where do they struggle? What workarounds do they build instead?
3
Built the Sample
Started with 16,000 names. Filtered down to a real, usable list. Caught that sales leaders were completely missing. Pushed to get them added so the sample was actually representative.

Final cohort: 38 sellers from three continents. Account Executives. Specialists. Leaders. An average of 5.6 years at Salesforce. Real perspectives across real roles.

4
Coordinated Globally
Ran sessions across three continents at three different times. Mixed recruiting sources. Made sure every region and role was represented. Kept everyone coordinated and on time.
5
Translated the Jargon
Jumped into live sessions to translate sales terminology on the fly. Sellers spoke one language. The product team spoke another. I made sure the feedback was actually understood correctly.
6
Pushed Upstream
Didn't just hand off a report. Proposed a workshop to map insights directly to roadmap priorities. Got UX into strategy conversations instead of just waiting for requirements.

Impact Across Salesforce

  • Started a Program
    This research became the template for the Quarterly Seller Research Program. Now it's a recurring thing. Every quarter has a focus and dedicated research. One project turned into a practice.
  • Changed the Roadmap
    Findings went straight to roadmap decisions. Territory intelligence. Insight logic. Sales leaders built their strategy on seller data instead of guessing.
  • Moved UX Upstream
    Instead of waiting for requirements, UX now sits in strategy conversations from the start. I proposed the workshop. I pushed the seat at the table. Now it's just how we work.
  • Recognized Work
    Called out in the Big Wins recap by peers. Portfolio-worthy. Spotted the gap. Built the right sample. Translated terminology. Proposed the workshop. Pushed the work upstream.

Why This Matters

Three things could have gone wrong if I hadn't owned this work:

  • Gap goes unaddressed
    Nobody else saw it. Nobody owned it. The March deadline arrives and we're building the FY27 roadmap blind.
  • Missing half the story
    I caught that sales leaders weren't in the original sample. Without them, we'd only understand how Account Executives work. Leadership uses planning differently. The sample would have been incomplete.
  • Data gets misunderstood
    Sellers use insider jargon. If the product team interprets it wrong, roadmap decisions get made on faulty data. Credibility dies. The whole research falls apart.
